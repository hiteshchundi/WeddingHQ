# Wedding HQ — Stitch Master Prompt

Design a cohesive, high-fidelity responsive web product called **Wedding HQ**.

Wedding HQ is a private wedding coordination platform designed initially for Indian weddings.

Its purpose is not to create a public wedding website.

It helps couples organise:

- Events
- Guests
- RSVPs
- Tasks
- Vendors
- Expenses
- Messages
- Wedding photos

while giving guests an extremely simple account-free experience.

The core product philosophy is:

> **Powerful for organisers. Extremely simple for guests.**

The primary V1 audience is **couples planning their wedding**.

Wedding planners and admins exist inside the application, but do not design the marketing site around professional planners yet.

---

# 1. Overall Design Goal

Create a wedding product that feels unlike traditional wedding websites.

The product must feel:

- Calm
- Warm
- Human
- Trustworthy
- Authentic
- Mature
- Thoughtful
- Contemporary
- Premium but understated
- Easy to understand
- Comfortable to use for long periods

It should feel like a product built by people who understand how chaotic wedding coordination can become.

The first emotional reaction should be:

> **This feels calm.**

Then:

> **This was clearly designed for people actually managing a wedding.**

Then:

> **I understand what this does.**

Finally:

> **I trust this enough to put my wedding information here.**

---

# 2. Design Personality

Use approximately:

**60% warm + human**  
**40% premium + understated**

The organiser workspace should have a romance/emotional level of approximately **3–4 out of 10**.

The guest experience may be warmer at approximately **5–6 out of 10**.

Do not turn the organiser workspace into a decorative wedding website.

Warmth should come from:

- Typography
- Couple names
- Language
- Whitespace
- Illustration
- Soft colour
- Thoughtful details

not from excessive ornament.

---

# 3. Important Visual Anti-Patterns

Do NOT use:

- Stock wedding photography
- Generic smiling couples
- Wedding ring stock images
- Generic Indian wedding stock photos
- Bright red backgrounds
- Hot pink-heavy interfaces
- Saturated marigold-yellow interfaces
- Floral borders everywhere
- Generic mandala decoration
- Decorative paisley everywhere
- Champagne imagery
- Script fonts
- Calligraphy fonts
- Neon colours
- Glassmorphism
- Huge gradients
- Generic blue SaaS styling
- Excessive rounded cards
- Huge drop shadows
- Floating-card overload
- Analytics-heavy dashboard layouts
- Multiple meaningless charts
- Enterprise software aesthetics
- Futuristic AI-product styling

This product should not resemble a wedding-template marketplace.

---

# 4. Core Colour System

Use a restrained palette based on:

- Warm ivory / off-white
- Soft charcoal
- Warm stone grey
- Muted sage / olive green
- Very small amounts of clay / terracotta

The **primary accent is muted sage / olive green**.

It should feel calm and grounded.

Do not turn it into bright emerald or neon green.

Clay / terracotta should be used mostly for:

- Illustration details
- Tiny decorative accents
- Occasional warm emotional details

It should not compete with sage as the primary UI accent.

---

# 5. Light Theme

Do not use harsh pure white as the dominant background.

Use:

- Warm ivory page background
- Soft warm-white or stone surfaces
- Charcoal primary text
- Warm-grey secondary text
- Muted sage actions
- Soft warm-grey borders

The design should feel comfortable enough to use for hours.

---

# 6. Dark Theme

Do not use pure black.

Dark mode should feel like:

> **The evening version of Wedding HQ.**

Use:

- Deep warm charcoal background
- Dark stone surfaces
- Warm off-white primary text
- Muted warm-grey secondary text
- Softened sage accent
- Very subtle warm borders

Do not make it resemble a developer dashboard or gaming interface.

---

# 7. Theme System

The product supports:

- Light
- Dark
- System

The organiser should have an appearance control.

The selected state should conceptually persist for the user.

---

# 8. Eye Comfort Mode

Also design a separate **Eye Comfort** mode inspired by the intention of Night Shift.

States:

- Off
- On
- Auto

This does NOT apply a strong yellow/orange filter.

Instead, Comfort Mode should:

- Slightly warm surfaces
- Reduce cool/blue undertones
- Slightly reduce decorative saturation
- Soften bright surfaces
- Soften borders
- Make dark mode slightly warmer
- Keep text highly readable

Core principle:

> **Low glare, not low readability.**

Show at least one screen demonstrating Dark + Comfort Mode.

---

# 9. Typography

Use two complementary type styles.

## Primary

A clean contemporary sans-serif.

Use for:

- Product interface
- Navigation
- Forms
- Buttons
- Tables
- Metrics
- Operational headings
- Body copy

It should feel warm and highly readable, not futuristic.

## Secondary

A tasteful editorial serif.

Use selectively for:

- Couple names
- Marketing hero headlines
- Guest wedding titles
- Emotional moments

Do not use serif heavily inside operational UI.

Do not use handwritten or wedding-calligraphy fonts.

---

# 10. Illustration Direction

Use original, hand-drawn editorial illustration.

Visual qualities:

- Minimal line work
- Graceful forms
- Strong negative space
- Human warmth
- Understated humour
- Indian cultural familiarity
- Elegant simplicity
- Restrained colour

Illustrations may contain subtle:

- Indian wedding details
- South-Indian details
- Telugu cultural character where relevant

Possible subjects:

- A family member holding a wedding checklist
- An older couple looking at a venue map on a phone
- Hands passing an invitation
- Wedding shoes near a doorway
- A camera beside scattered guest photographs
- Guests arriving at a venue
- Chairs waiting for a ceremony
- Someone coordinating wedding tasks

Do NOT reproduce, trace, or imitate Bapu or any other specific artist.

Create an original illustration language based only on broad principles such as:

- Expressive line work
- Simplicity
- Indian cultural familiarity
- Graceful composition
- Understatement

Avoid caricature.

---

# 11. Illustration Colour

Use softened colours.

For example:

- Marigold → muted ochre
- Bright red → earthy brick
- Hot pink → dusty rose
- Emerald → muted sage/forest
- Royal blue → muted indigo

Illustrations must work comfortably in both light and dark themes.

---

# 12. Surface Design

Create a slightly tactile editorial feel without skeuomorphism.

Use:

- Warm paper-like surfaces
- Fine borders
- Generous spacing
- Restrained corner radius
- Very subtle shadows only when hierarchy requires them
- Calm editorial composition

Avoid making every item a floating card.

---

# 13. Product Copy Tone

Use language that is:

- Human
- Short
- Plain
- Warm
- Reassuring

Avoid technical and enterprise language.

For example:

Use:

> **Bring your guest list with you.**

Not:

> Import guest dataset.

Use:

> **83 photos are waiting for review.**

Not:

> Pending moderation queue: 83.

# 49. Information Hierarchy Rule

Every screen must clearly answer:

1. Where am I?
2. What matters most?
3. What needs my attention?
4. What can I do next?

# 50. Final Creative Direction

The finished experience should feel like:

> **A beautifully organised wedding notebook transformed into modern software.**

It should combine:

- The clarity and calmness of excellent productivity software
- The warmth and humanity of a wedding
- The authenticity of an Indian context
- The restraint of thoughtful editorial design

without becoming:

- A traditional wedding website
- A wedding invitation template
- A generic SaaS dashboard
- An enterprise project-management tool

The product should feel authentic enough that a couple opening it thinks:

> **Yes. This is where our wedding belongs.**
